## A. Today in one paragraph

The page looks like a careful default. It is a white-and-warm-grey admin page in the system font with one stock blue (`#2b5cff`), rounded white cards with hairline borders, and pill-shaped status chips in green, amber and red. Nothing is ugly and the words are good: plain English is already the strongest part of the design. But nothing visual says "Lorefile" either. Remove the logo and it could be any SaaS settings screen. The one thing with real character is the isometric Overview drawing (the tilted plane with tiles per entity), and it lives on a single tab, unrelated in style to every other screen. Underneath, the look was grown screen by screen rather than designed once: there are 15 text sizes between 10 and 24 px, 49 different padding values, 9 different shadows and 323 inline `style=` attributes in the JavaScript. That is why small things disagree: three panels fold three different ways, the same toolbar sits in different places on neighbouring tabs, and text boxes open at 16 px tall. The honesty features (grades, "nobody has graded this yet", "ungraded") are the product's real point of view, and visually they are small grey pills that are easy to miss.

## C. Every inconsistency

Severity: **high** = something is broken or unreadable; **medium** = people notice and it slows them; **low** = polish.

Your four issues: issue 1 → UI-01 to UI-06. Issue 2 → UI-07, UI-08, UI-09. Issue 3 → UI-13 to UI-19. Issue 4 → UI-24, UI-27, UI-31 and section D.

### Panel controls

**UI-01 · Three panels, three fold controls** (high) — *Vinay's issue 1*
- **What's wrong.** The left menu folds with a bordered button that says "« Fold", at the very bottom. The list column folds with a bare "«" at its top right. The right panel folds with a bare "»" at its top right. Folded, the menu shows a bordered "»" button at the bottom, while the other two show a tall clickable strip with a chevron at the top.
- **Root cause.** Three separate pieces of code were written for the three panels:
  - Menu: `renderNav`, line 599, a `button.small.foldbtn` appended after everything else in the menu, styled at line 119.
  - List: `listCol`, line 559, a `button.colbtn` (line 129) as the first child, plus a `.strip`.
  - Right panel: `asideHead`, line 561, a `.colbtn` inside `.asidehead`, which needs a negative margin (line 135) to sit right, plus `asideStrip`, line 562.
- **Evidence.** `desktop-entities.png` (the "«" above "+ New entity"), `desktop-open-nav-scrolled-to-fold.png` ("« Fold" bottom left), `desktop-folded-home.png` (strip with "«" at the top right, "»" button bottom left).
- **Fix: one "panel head" pattern** (design system Part 6). Every panel gets the same 36px head row: the panel's name in small capitals (Ledger's names: **CONTENTS** for the menu, **ENTITIES** or whatever the list holds, **NOTES** for the right panel), and the same icon button in the head's corner facing the reading column (top right on Contents and the list, top left on Notes). Folded, every panel becomes a rail (44px; Contents 56px because it keeps its icons) that is one button, with the same icon at the same top position and the panel's name written down it. One helper, `panelHead(key, name, keyHint)`, replaces `foldbtn`, `colbtn` and `asidehead`. One drawn icon (a panel-with-arrow glyph added to `GLYPHS`), not "«" text. Tooltips say "Hide contents ( [ )" / "Show contents", "Hide list ( \ )" / "Show list", "Hide notes ( ] )" / "Show notes".

**UI-02 · The menu's Fold button is usually off-screen** (high)
- **What's wrong.** On a 1440×900 screen the menu is taller than the window, so "« Fold" sits below the fold. You have to scroll the menu to find the control that hides it. On a laptop the folded "»" button is half hidden behind the footer.
- **Where.** `renderNav` line 599 puts it last; measured at y=890 in a 900px-tall window where the footer starts at 866.
- **Evidence.** `desktop-home.png` (no Fold visible), `desktop-open-nav-scrolled-to-fold.png`, `laptop-entities.png` (the "»" box cut by the footer).
- **Fix.** UI-01 moves it to the top. Nothing else needed.

**UI-03 · Keyboard shortcuts cover two panels of three** (low)
- **What's wrong.** `[` folds the menu and `]` folds the right panel (lines 573–574). The list column has no key. Only the right panel's tooltip mentions its key.
- **Fix.** Add `\` for the list. Every tooltip shows its key in the same format: "Hide list ( \ )".

**UI-04 · Folded widths and behaviours differ** (medium)
- **What's wrong.** The menu folds to 56px and stays useful (icons). The list and right panel fold to 40px and show only a name. In the folded menu only the small button reopens it; in the other two the whole strip is clickable. The strips answer to Enter but not Space (lines 559, 562), so they are not proper buttons for keyboard users.
- **Fix.** One rail width token (`--rail:44px`; the menu may keep 56 because it holds icons). The whole rail is a real `<button>` in all three. Space and Enter both work for free.

**UI-05 · Folding the right panel on Guardrails doesn't hide its content** (high)
- **What's wrong.** Fold the right panel on Guardrails and the selected guardrail's text stays, crushed into a 40px column, one syllable per line.
- **Root cause.** The fold rule hides the panel's children with `display:none` (line 134). The selected-guardrail block carries an inline `style="display:flex;flex-direction:column;gap:6px"`, and inline styles beat stylesheet rules. I confirmed it in the live page: that one child reports `display:flex` while all its siblings report `none`.
- **Evidence.** `desktop-folded-rules.png`, `laptop-folded-rules.png`.
- **Fix.** Wrap each panel's content in one `<div class="panel-body">` and hide that single element when folded. Replace that inline style with the `.col` class. Longer term, remove inline `display` styles altogether (UI-25).

**UI-06 · Panels only auto-fold for the menu** (medium)
- **What's wrong.** Below 1100px the menu folds by itself until you choose (line 545). The list and the 360px right panel never do, so at 1024 and 768 the middle column is starved (see UI-13). And once you click any fold button, your choice for all three is saved, so the menu stops adapting to window width forever.
- **Fix.** Keep a saved choice per panel only when the person made it for that panel. Without a saved choice, each panel follows the width tier in UI-13.

### Form fields

**UI-07 · Text boxes, guardrail cards and menu rows are squashed** (high) — *Vinay's issue 2, root cause*
- **What's wrong.** On an entity page the description box opens 16px tall, showing the top sliver of one line. Its content needs 79px. You have to drag it open. The same bug clips every guardrail card to 34px when it needs 58px, so the second line ("whenever an action touches…") is cut in half or overlaps the next card. In the left menu, "Sources" overflows its row.
- **Root cause.** `.main` (line 38), `nav` (line 26), `.aside` (line 42) and `.list` (line 39) are scrolling columns built with `display:flex; flex-direction:column; overflow:auto`. In a flex column, children are allowed to shrink when the content is taller than the box. Most children refuse to shrink below their content, but three kinds do not refuse: a `<textarea>`, anything with `overflow:auto` (like `<pre>`), and any `<button>`, because line 13 gives every button `min-height:36px`, which replaces the "don't shrink below my content" default. Guardrail cards are `<button class="card">` (line 2420), and menu rows are buttons with `min-height:42px` (line 29). So the taller the page, the more these get crushed. That is why it looks random: short pages are fine.
- **Measured.** Description textarea: `rows=2`, height 16px, content 79px. Guardrail cards: height 34px, content 58px, `flex-shrink:1`, `min-height:36px`.
- **Evidence.** `desktop-entities.png` (the sliver under "Order"), `desktop-rules.png` (cut second lines), `tablet-rules.png` (cards overlapping badly), `desktop-sources.png` ("Sources" row in the menu).
- **Fix.** One line: `.main > *, nav > *, .aside > *, .list > * { flex-shrink:0 }`. Before: description box 16px tall. After: two full lines, as `rows="2"` intended.

**UI-08 · Text boxes don't grow with what's in them** (high) — *Vinay's issue 2, second half*
- **What's wrong.** Even once UI-07 is fixed, the Order description is three lines in a two-line box. Every textarea is a fixed number of rows (`rows="2"` at lines 1969, 2084, 2630) and relies on the drag handle (`resize:vertical`, line 19).
- **Fix.** One shared class, `.autogrow`, on every textarea: `field-sizing:content; min-height:2lh; max-height:40vh`. `field-sizing` is new CSS that sizes a box to its text; Chrome has it, and I have not checked Safari and Firefox, so add a 6-line fallback that sets the height from `scrollHeight` when the page draws and on each keystroke. Remove the drag handle once boxes fit themselves.

**UI-09 · Field descriptions are one-line boxes that cut the sentence** (high)
- **What's wrong.** The most important text on an entity page, the plain-words sentence for each field, sits in a single-line `<input>` in the last table column. At 1440px that column is 206px wide and shows "Customer who placed it; see" then stops. At 1024px it is about 70px ("Order r", "Custom"). At 768px the column is off the right edge of the screen.
- **Root cause.** Line 2065. The table's "kind" cell has an inline `white-space:nowrap`, so one long enum ("enum · placed / paid / packing / shipped / delivered / cancelled / returned") sets that column's width, and the description column gets what's left. And an `<input>` can never wrap.
- **Evidence.** `desktop-entities.png`, `laptop-entities.png`, `tablet-entities.png`, `phone-entities.png`.
- **Fix.** Replace the table with a "field row" pattern: line 1 is the field name (monospace) with its kind beside it, wrapping allowed; line 2 is the description as a full-width `.autogrow` textarea. On wide screens it may sit in two columns (name and kind left at a fixed 240px, description right, taking the rest). Enum values over 4 items shorten to "enum · 7 values" with the list on hover and in the YAML.

**UI-10 · Saving is invisible** (medium)
- **What's wrong.** Descriptions save when you click away (`onchange="patch(…)"`, 3 places). There is no Save button and no "saved" mark next to the box; feedback is a toast in the far corner. For a page whose job is trust, a person should see that their sentence was kept. *Not fully tested: I read the code path but did not time the toast.*
- **Fix.** A small "Saved ✓" / "Saving…" / "Not saved: reason" note under the box that just changed. Same pattern everywhere a field saves by itself.

**UI-11 · Inputs, buttons and selects are different heights** (low)
- **What's wrong.** Inputs come out 37px, buttons 36px, the header picker 32px, the Home find box 32px, small buttons 30px. Side by side they don't line up (`desktop-entity-new-form.png`: "Create it" and "Cancel" beside each other are fine, but name box vs Save on Settings differ).
- **Where.** Lines 13, 14, 16, 23, 246.
- **Fix.** `--h-control` on all three element types; `--h-compact` for the `.small` variant of all three.

**UI-12 · A disabled button dressed as a text box** (medium)
- **What's wrong.** Next to "ungraded" there is a pale bordered box reading "Type your name first". It looks like an empty input you should type into. It is actually the disabled "Looks right" button with its label swapped (`nameBtn`, line 368).
- **Evidence.** `desktop-entities.png`, `desktop-logic.png`, `desktop-rules.png`.
- **Fix.** Keep the real label ("Looks right"), disabled, with a link beside it: "Add your name to grade". The link focuses the name box. Also, form labels come in three styles (`.form label` 12.5px at line 61; inline 12px labels in the Guardrails panel; hidden labels): make one `.label` class.

### Responsive and breakpoints

**UI-13 · There is no tablet or small-laptop layout** (high) — *Vinay's issue 3, root cause A*
- **What's wrong.** From 761px up to any width, the page is always "menu + list + main + right panel" side by side. Only the menu shrinks. At 768px (an upright iPad) Home's main column is 352px between a 56px rail and a 360px right panel, and the Overview drawing is shown at 21% zoom with unreadable labels. On Guardrails at 768 the cards are crushed and overlap.
- **Root cause.** One layout breakpoint, 760px (5 CSS blocks and `phone()` at line 696). `.aside` is a fixed `flex:0 0 360px` (line 42) and `.list` a fixed 240px (line 39) at every width above it.
- **Evidence.** `tablet-home.png`, `tablet-rules.png`, `tablet-entities.png`, `w900-*.png`.
- **Fix: four deliberate tiers** (design system Part 5, where the menu is called Contents, the list column the Index, and the right panel Notes), used by CSS and by one JS helper `tier()` that replaces `phone()`:

**UI-41 · Input borders barely show** (low)

**UI-43 · Text in the drawing goes down to 10px, then gets scaled** (low)

## D. Four layout directions

All four keep every screen and feature the page has today. They change where things sit and how the page looks. All four use the fixes in section C; none of them is a way around those.

**A note on fonts, because the page works offline.** Two honest options:
- **System fonts**: zero bytes, already on the machine. Modern systems ship good ones beyond the default: a serif (`ui-serif` gives New York on Apple; `"Iowan Old Style", "Palatino Linotype", Georgia` elsewhere), a monospace (`ui-monospace`), a rounded face on Apple. The catch: it looks a little different on Mac, Windows and Linux.
- **Embedded fonts**: a font file pasted into `index.html` as text (base64). Looks identical everywhere. The catch is weight: I estimate 30–70 KB per font for Latin letters only, and the binary grows by that much. Only open-licence fonts (SIL OFL) should be used. I have not measured these; treat the sizes as estimates.

### Direction 1 · Ledger

**The idea.** The page is the business's record book: it reads like a well-kept document, and every statement carries a visible mark of who stands behind it.

**Before / after.** Today the Order page is a form: boxes inside a table. In Ledger it is a page you read: "**Order** — One checkout. Status follows the parcel…" as real text, with a small pencil beside each sentence. Click the sentence and it becomes editable right where it is.

**Shell**
- **Header.** One slim line: logo, space picker, mode pill, "⋯". (As UI-16.)
- **Left menu.** A "contents" column, like a book's table of contents: Build as numbered chapters 1–6, Use below. 220px, folds to an icon rail.
- **List column.** Stays, as a narrow index (entity names grouped by area). On Guardrails and Logic the list of items moves into this same column, so every Build screen works the same way: index on the left, one item's page on the right.
- **Right panel.** Becomes a "margin": notes about the item you're reading (who owns it, its grade and history, what to do next). 300px.
- **One collapse pattern.** The panel head from UI-01: a title and one fold icon at the top of each column; folded, a 44px rail with the name written vertically. All three identical.

**Entity page and its form.** A single reading column, at most 720px wide.
- Title, then the description as a paragraph you click to edit (it turns into an auto-growing box with Save / Cancel and "Saved ✓").
- Fields as a definition list: `order_id` in monospace with its kind beside it in grey, then its sentence underneath as text. Click to edit. An undescribed field shows a dashed amber line: "Not described yet. What does placed_on mean?"
- Links, Guardrails, Data and YAML stay as tabs.
- Area, owner and "also called" move to the margin panel.

**Graph and Overview.** Unchanged in structure. They are drawn as a plate in the book: a ruled frame, a caption underneath ("Figure: everything in ecommerce"), the legend as a key in the corner. Tiles become paper-coloured with ink outlines.

**Tablet (768).** Contents folds to its rail. Index and margin become drawers over the reading column. The reading column is comfortable at 768 because it never wanted more than 720.

**Phone (375).** One column. "Menu" opens the contents. The index is its own screen. The margin's notes sit under the title as a fold-out "About this entity".

**Character**
- **Type.** A serif for titles and for the sentences people write; a clean sans for controls; monospace for YAML names. System-font version (0 KB): `ui-serif, "Iowan Old Style", "Palatino Linotype", Georgia, serif` with the current sans. Embedded version: Source Serif 4 for the serif (est. 50–70 KB) and keep the system sans.
- **Colour.** Paper `#FAF7F0`, card `#FFFDF8`, ink `#1F1B16`, second ink `#5C544A` (6.9:1), line `#E4DCCB`, strong line `#8F8573`. Accent oxblood `#8C2F1B` (7.7:1 on paper; white on it 8.3:1), accent tint `#F6E7E1`. Checked green `#2F6B3A`, look-at-it amber `#8A5200`, stop red `#A3231A`, accepted ink-blue `#2B4C7E`. All above 5.9:1 on paper.
- **Shape.** Nearly square: 4px on controls, 6px on cards. Hairline rules instead of card boxes where possible.
- **Density.** Roomy. Body text 15px in the reading column, line height 1.6.
- **Signature detail: the stamp.** Each grade is drawn as an ink stamp in the margin beside the statement it belongs to, not a grey pill. *Ungraded* is an empty dashed outline. *Proposed* is a pencil-grey "AGENT WROTE · NOT REVIEWED". *Accepted* is a blue stamp with a name and date. *Checked* is a green stamp "CHECKED AGAINST ROWS · Priya · 28 Nov". You can scan a page's left margin and see at a glance what is and isn't verified.

**Token sketch**
```css
--bg:#FAF7F0; --panel:#FFFDF8; --ink:#1F1B16; --muted:#5C544A; --line:#E4DCCB; --line-strong:#8F8573;
--accent:#8C2F1B; --accent-soft:#F6E7E1; --ok:#2F6B3A; --warn:#8A5200; --block:#A3231A; --accepted:#2B4C7E;
--serif:ui-serif,"Iowan Old Style","Palatino Linotype",Georgia,serif;
--r-control:4px; --r-card:6px; --fs-body:15px; --fs-h1:30px; --measure:720px;
```

**Suits.** Business owners and ops leads reviewing what agents wrote: people who read. It makes plain English and honest status the look itself.
**Trade-offs.** Lower density: builders editing 40 fields scroll more. Serif system fonts differ between Mac and Windows unless a font is embedded. Click-to-edit needs care so it is obvious text is editable (pencil icon, hover line).

### Direction 2 · Workbench

**The idea.** A dense tool for builders, laid out like a mail app or code editor: panes you can drag wider or narrower, and a command bar for everything.

**Before / after.** Today, to find "Refund order" you pick a menu item, then scan. In Workbench you press Ctrl/Cmd-K, type "refund", and choose from entities, guardrails, actions and commands in one list.

**Shell**
- **Header.** Replaced by a 40px top bar: space picker left, a wide "Search or jump to…" bar in the middle (the menu's Find box and Home's Find merged into one), mode pill and "⋯" right.
- **Left menu.** A permanent 48px icon rail with tooltips; labels appear in a flyout on hover or when pinned open. Status dots on the icons.
- **List column.** Always present on every Build and Use screen as pane 2 (entities, guardrails, metrics, people, files, history entries). A filter box at its top.
- **Right panel.** Pane 4, an "inspector": properties of whatever is selected in pane 3.
- **One collapse pattern.** Every border between panes is a drag handle. Drag to resize; double-click the handle, or click the small tab on it, to collapse that pane to nothing. A collapsed pane leaves the same small tab on the edge to bring it back. Same handle, three places. Widths are remembered.

**Entity page and its form.** A grid, like a spreadsheet: one row per field, columns for name, kind, description. The description cell wraps and grows. Tab and arrow keys move between cells; Enter edits. Beside the grid, an optional "YAML twin" pane (see signature).

**Graph and Overview.** A tab in pane 3. The inspector shows the selected node, so the drawing no longer needs its own card panel.

**Tablet (768).** Rail + list + detail; the inspector becomes a drawer. Handles become fixed.
**Phone (375).** Honestly weak. It falls back to list → detail screens, and the grid becomes stacked rows. This direction is designed for a laptop.

**Character**
- **Type.** One sans plus monospace, with the monospace used generously (names, ids, counts). System version: current stack. Embedded version: IBM Plex Sans + IBM Plex Mono (est. 60–90 KB for both, regular and semibold).
- **Colour.** Cool and neutral. Background `#F3F4F6`, panel `#FFFFFF`, ink `#111418`, second ink `#5A6370` (5.5:1), line `#DDE1E6`, strong line `#7B8491`. Primary buttons are ink-black (white on it 18:1). One highlight: amber `#FFC53D` as a fill for "needs you" (ink on it 11.7:1), with `#8A5A00` when amber is text.
- **Shape.** 4px everywhere. No shadows except menus.
- **Density.** Tight: 13px body, 28px rows.
- **Signature detail: the YAML twin.** Every form shows, live, the exact YAML lines it will write, highlighted as you type. It says what the product is (files) and never hides what a click does.

**Token sketch**
```css
--bg:#F3F4F6; --panel:#FFFFFF; --ink:#111418; --muted:#5A6370; --line:#DDE1E6; --line-strong:#7B8491;
--accent:#111418; --attention:#FFC53D; --attention-text:#8A5A00;
--r-control:4px; --r-card:4px; --fs-body:13px; --h-control:28px; --rail:48px;
```

**Suits.** Builders describing a business with 50+ entities, people who live in editors.
**Trade-offs.** It is the opposite of plain English for owners: dense, technical, YAML up front. Resizable panes are the most code of the four. Weak on phones. Risks looking like "another dev tool", which is generic in a different way.

### Direction 3 · Atlas

**The idea.** The isometric drawing is the product's face, so make it the whole page: the map is always there, and everything else opens as a sheet on top of it.

**Before / after.** Today the Overview is one tab on one screen, and clicking "Open Order" throws the map away for a form. In Atlas you click the Order tile and a wide sheet slides in from the right covering two-thirds of the screen; the map stays visible at the left, with Order's tile lit. Close the sheet and you are back where you were.

**Shell**
- **Header.** None as a bar. A small floating card at the top left: logo, space picker, mode pill.
- **Left menu.** A floating vertical dock of icons on the left edge (the folded menu, always). Hover or click shows labels. Build steps show progress as a ring around each icon.
- **List column.** Becomes the map's "List" view and a search box. Inside a sheet, a small "next / previous in this area" control replaces the list.
- **Right panel.** The sheet. Three sizes: peek (360px, today's card), half, and full.
- **One collapse pattern.** One grab handle on the sheet's edge: drag or click to step between peek, half, full and closed. The dock has no collapse, because it is already minimal. One moving part on the whole page.

**Entity page and its form.** Inside the sheet at half or full size: a header band in the area's colour with the entity's tile drawn in it, then description, fields as field rows (UI-09), and tabs. Non-map screens (People, History, Data & SQL, Settings, Files) are full-size sheets with the map dimmed behind.

**Graph and Overview.** Overview is the home canvas. Graph is a mode of the same canvas: pick an entity and the plane dissolves into the centred graph. One stage, two arrangements.

**Tablet (768).** The dock stays. Sheets open full width with a "Map" button to go back.
**Phone (375).** The map cannot be the base at 375px, so it flips: the List is home, and the map is a tab. The direction loses most of its point on a phone.

**Character**
- **Type.** A geometric sans with some personality for labels and titles; system sans for long text. Embedded: Space Grotesk for titles only (est. 30–40 KB), or system-only with wide letter-spaced caps as on the plane today.
- **Colour.** The ground of the map is the page: `#EEF1EC` (a grey with a little green), panel `#FFFFFF`, ink `#16201B`, second ink `#55615A` (5.7:1), line `#D8DED6`. Accent deep teal `#0F766E` (5.5:1 on white). The eight area colours carry into sheets, so "Customers & orders" is blue everywhere. Teal would need to leave the area palette (UI-30).
- **Shape.** Soft and layered: 14px sheets, 10px controls, real shadows, because things float.
- **Density.** Medium inside sheets, sparse on the map.
- **Signature detail: the tile.** Each entity's isometric tile is its identity everywhere: in the sheet header, in lists, in search results, in the review screen. Its edge colour is its area; its badge is its status.
- Optional: a dark stage for the map only (UI-37 option 3).

**Token sketch**
```css
--bg:#EEF1EC; --panel:#FFFFFF; --ink:#16201B; --muted:#55615A; --line:#D8DED6;
--accent:#0F766E; --accent-soft:#E0F0EC;
--r-control:10px; --r-card:14px; --shadow-pop:0 8px 28px rgba(22,32,27,.14);
--sheet-peek:360px; --sheet-half:56vw; --sheet-full:calc(100vw - 72px);
```

**Suits.** Demos, first impressions, people who think in pictures. It is the most "this is Lorefile" of the four at a glance.
**Trade-offs.** Most work happens in forms and lists, and here they are guests on top of a drawing. A space with 80 entities makes a crowded map as the permanent backdrop. Sheets over a canvas are hard to get right for keyboard and screen-reader users. Weakest on phones. Highest build risk.

### Direction 4 · Review desk

**The idea.** Organise the page around the job people come to do: look at what agents wrote, compare before and after, and decide.

**Before / after.** Today Home shows the map, with "What to do next" as three small cards in the right panel, and proposals live under Use → Agents. In Review desk the first thing you see is the queue: "3 proposals from agents · 47 definitions nobody has graded · 12 entities with undescribed fields", and the first item open beside it, as before on the left and after on the right, with Accept / Set aside under it.

**Shell**
- **Header.** Becomes the navigation. Two lines: line 1 has logo, space picker, mode pill, "⋯". Line 2 has five top tabs: **Review** (the queue), **Build ▾** (Sources, Entities, Links, Logic, Guardrails, Actions, shown as a six-step progress line you can click), **Map** (Overview / Graph / List), **Use ▾** (How to use it, Agents, People, History, Data & SQL, Files), **Settings**.
- **Left menu.** None. The 240px goes back to content.
- **List column.** The left of a two-column layout, 320px: the queue on Review, the entity list on Entities, and so on. Each row is two lines: name, then why it needs you.
- **Right panel.** Folded into the main column as sections. No third column.
- **One collapse pattern.** Only the list column can fold, with the UI-01 panel head. One control on the whole page.

**Entity page and its form.** Card-based, one field per card: name and kind as the card's head, the sentence as an auto-growing box, and a footer line that is always present: "agent wrote this · nobody has checked" or "accepted by Vinay · 27 Nov". When an agent proposed a change, the card splits in two: before | after.

**Graph and Overview.** Under the Map tab, full width (no side menu to share with). The selected item's card opens as a drawer.

**Tablet (768).** Top tabs fit. The list column becomes a drawer. Before/after stack vertically, before on top.
**Phone (375).** Top tabs become a bottom bar with five icons (Review, Build, Map, Use, Settings). Queue → item → decision, one screen each. The strongest of the four on a phone.

**Character**
- **Type.** A humanist sans (friendlier letter shapes than the system default) with clear numerals. Embedded: Atkinson Hyperlegible (est. 40–60 KB for two weights), designed for legibility; or system sans with larger sizes.
- **Colour.** White page `#FFFFFF`, second surface `#F5F5F2`, ink `#15151A`, second ink `#62626C` (6:1), line `#E5E5E0`. Accent signal orange `#B93C0A` (5.7:1 on white, white on it 5.7:1). "Before" tint `#FBEDEA`, "after" tint `#E9F5EC`.
- **Shape.** 10px cards, pill buttons for the two decisions (Accept, Set aside).
- **Density.** Medium, generous in the reading area.
- **Signature detail: the seam.** Every change, anywhere, is shown the same way: two half-cards side by side with a vertical seam between them, before on the left in a warm tint with strike-through, after on the right in a green tint. It appears in the queue, in History, on entity pages and on the Graph. Combined with the always-present status footer on each card.

**Token sketch**
```css
--bg:#FFFFFF; --panel:#FFFFFF; --surface-2:#F5F5F2; --ink:#15151A; --muted:#62626C; --line:#E5E5E0;
--accent:#B93C0A; --before:#FBEDEA; --after:#E9F5EC;
--r-control:999px; --r-card:10px; --list-w:320px; --fs-body:15px;
```

**Suits.** Owners and ops or marketing leads whose only job on the page is to review. Teams with active agents.
**Trade-offs.** A space with no agents connected yet has an empty Review tab, so first-run needs its own design ("Describe your business" as the first queue). Thirteen destinations behind two dropdown menus are one click further than today's always-visible menu. Builders lose the at-a-glance Build checklist unless the progress line is done well. It moves Home, which the home-plane spec made the landing screen, so it changes a standing decision.

### Recommendation

**What was decided:** Ledger, with Workbench's header search only (see the top of this spec). The recommendation as it was written before the choice follows.

**Direction 1, Ledger, with two things borrowed.**

Why Ledger:
1. **It fits who uses the page.** Owners and ops leads come to read and vouch. A page that reads like a record is easier for them than a tool (2) or a canvas (3).
2. **Its signature is the product's promise.** "Honest about what is and isn't verified" is the thing you keep asking for. The stamp makes that the most visible element on every page, where today it is a grey pill.
3. **It is the cheapest real change.** The shell stays menu · list · page · side panel, so sections C's fixes carry straight over, and nothing in the specs has to be re-decided. System serif fonts cost zero bytes, so the single-file offline rule holds without a trade-off.
4. **It is not generic.** Warm paper, a serif, square corners and ink stamps look like nothing else in this category, which is mostly blue rounded SaaS.

What to borrow:
- From **Review desk**: the before/after seam for anything an agent proposed, and a "needs you" queue as the first section of Home (above the drawing, not replacing it).
- From **Workbench**: Ctrl/Cmd-K "jump to anything", merging the two Find boxes.

What I would not do: Atlas as the base. The drawing is the best-looking thing on the page, but most of the time on the page is spent reading and writing sentences, and Atlas makes that the guest. Keep the drawing as Ledger's "plate".

**Suggested order of work**
1. The section C fixes that are direction-proof: UI-07, UI-20, UI-05, UI-01/02, UI-09, UI-08, then the tiers in UI-13 to UI-18. These are worth doing whichever direction wins.
2. The tokens in B12, replacing raw values screen by screen (UI-23, 24, 25, 27).
3. Ledger's palette, type and stamp on one screen first (the Order entity page), as a preview for you to judge before it spreads.
